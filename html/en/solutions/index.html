    Story

    Starting from culture: native terracotta memory and the philosophy of “breathing architecture”

    01. Rooted in Cultural Identity: The Soul of Vietnamese Terracotta and Native Microclimate Wisdom

    Throughout Vietnamese architectural history, terracotta and ceramics have been a cultural memory spanning millennia. Combined with native wisdom, they formed natural “breathing” envelopes such as perforated brick walls, bamboo screens and slatted doors that block heat, welcome breeze and filter sunlight poetically. Yet when identity is brought into contemporary buildings, over-reliance on mass-moulded industrial materials such as concrete or synthetic resin falls into the rut of copying ancient forms without soul, losing the rustic charm and living emotion of genuine terracotta.

    02. Carrying Heritage Forward Digitally: A Closed-Loop Parametric Design and 3D-Printed Ceramic Process

    The closed-loop 3D-printed ceramic facade process runs in four stages. It starts at the Đông Xưởng R&D centre, turning native cultural rules and climate data into parametric algorithms and G-code. Nam Xưởng follows with microclimate simulation and BIM engineering, including a dry-hung 304 stainless-steel frame. At Tây Xưởng, a robotic clay-extrusion arm (LDM) builds up ceramic blanks precisely with no moulds, then hand glazing and 1200°C firing create one-of-a-kind kiln-transformed components, before Nam Xưởng and Bắc Xưởng coordinate supply and complete installation nationwide.

    Research & Design

    Starting from decoding cultural heritage and natural laws, from the rhythm of water waves and terraced fields to tropical bamboo screens and marine micro-structures, the Đông xưởng R&D centre turns native memory into parametric design algorithms and non-linear G-code. Through BIM-standard digital computation, the system optimises all 5 core product lines at once: self-shading hollow structures for sun louvres, 1200°C glaze-flow direction for colour-shift tiles, mould-free relief surface variation, freed-up form for artwork, and programmed neutral-pH porous micro-cavities for coral reefs. This is the foundation that carries traditional terracotta straight into a precise, sustainable era of futuristic architecture.

    Production & Installation

    From the modelling data, Tây xưởng uses a robotic ceramic-paste extrusion arm (LDM) driven by G-code with zero tooling cost, making a 1:1 mock-up possible in 48–72 hours before glazing and sintering at 1200°C. After kiln transformation, ceramic structures meeting the A1 fire-resistance standard are handed to Nam xưởng and Bắc xưởng for dry installation on a frame and 304 stainless-steel bracket system. Mechanical assembly coordinated to BIM standards ensures resistance to storm wind loads, eliminates tolerance errors and optimises on-site completion time.

    Casa⁺’s Ecosystem of 5 Core 3D-Printed Ceramic Product Groups

    FACADE: 3D-Printed Ceramic Sun Louvres

    Programmed 3D hollow lattice that shades itself, cuts west-facing heat radiation, cools passively by evaporation, and dry-fits onto a 304 stainless-steel frame

    FACADE: Colour-Shift Glazed Tiles

    G-code controls groove depth to guide vitrified glaze flow at 1200°C (Painting with Shapes), creating a multi-dimensional gradient wall mural.

    FACADE: Relief Tiles

    Turn a project’s cultural story into a parametric bas-relief wall assembled from hundreds of varying modules with zero mould cost

    Ceramic Artwork & Decor

    3D printing in ceramic production widens what can be shaped: complex structures, free-form curves and fine detail. From household items and decorative objects to works of art, the technology supports personalised design, experiments with new forms and the development of one-of-a-kind pieces that traditional moulding struggles to make.

    3D-Printed Coral Reef

    Biomimicry of fungi and coral into a multi-tier porous terracotta structure with neutral pH, helping 95% of corals survive.
